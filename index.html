Septic Systems

How a septic system works

Every private sewage system does two jobs. A tank settles out the solids. Then the liquid, called effluent, goes to the soil, where it is treated as it soaks down. The soil on your land decides which kind of system you need.

Which system fits?

SystemUsually a fit whenKeep in mind
Treatment fieldDeep, suitable soil with plenty of roomLowest cost, but needs the most good soil
At-gradeSome good soil, but not enough for trenchesSits on the surface, needs pressure distribution
MoundShallow limiting layer or heavy clayRaised sand bed, more material and a pump
Secondary treatmentSmall lot, high water table or near waterCleaner effluent, but the unit needs regular service
Holding tankNo soil option on site, or very low useNo field at all, but it must be pumped out often

This is a general guide. The final choice comes from the test pits on your property.

Built to the Standard

Alberta Private Sewage Systems Standard of Practice 2021

The Standard sets the rules for how private sewage systems in Alberta are sized, placed and built. Every design I hand over shows the work, so the inspector and installer can check it.

  • Soil

    Texture and structure described for each horizon in the test pit

  • Limiting layer

    Depth to mottles, water table or bedrock recorded and used

  • Separation

    Vertical separation to the limiting layer checked for the treatment level

  • Sizing

    Daily flow, loading rates and field size worked out on the worksheets

  • Distribution

    Pressure distribution and dose volumes calculated where needed

  • Setbacks

    Distances to wells, buildings, property lines and water checked on the plan

About

A local designer who reads the soil first

Hi, I'm Maria Lee. I started Wild Rose Country Septic in Rimbey to help rural families, acreage owners and builders in central Alberta get septic systems that fit their land and pass inspection the first time.

My background is in administration and health, safety and environment (HSE) work. That taught me to keep careful records, follow the rules closely and explain them in plain words. I bring the same habits to every site I assess.

I focus on design and leave the digging of the system to certified installers. That keeps my advice honest. My only job is to find the right system for your property.

How I work

  • Soil first. No design before the test pits are dug and logged.
  • Clear paperwork. Drawings and worksheets an inspector can check line by line.
  • Plain answers. You will know why your system was chosen and what it costs to keep up.
